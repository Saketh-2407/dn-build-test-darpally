## LinkedIn post

Look at what the companies selling AI models paid for in 2026, and you'll see where AI projects get stuck.

On July 2, 2026, Microsoft launched Frontier, backed by $2.5 billion and roughly 6,000 engineers, The Next Web reported. Their job was to sit inside customers' companies and make the AI those customers already paid for work. The companies selling the models put money into people because customers who got the model alone did not see it in their numbers.

MIT's NANDA researchers found in 2025 that 95% of enterprise generative-AI pilots showed no measurable profit-and-loss impact, across more than 300 deployments. If your pilot got applause in the demo and nothing on the bottom line, look at the work around the tool.

Your team can do four jobs the model can't: name the job, fit the workflow, own the handoff, prove the number. Start by deciding what a win looks like before you switch the tool on.

Outside engineers can jumpstart a stalled rollout. If your people never learn the four jobs themselves, you've bought a dependency that walks out the door when the contract ends. Buy the help, keep the muscle.
